Applying to the College of Agriculture and Life Sciences

In the Cornell University College of Agriculture and Life Sciences (CALS), purpose-driven science drives everything we do. We work across disciplines to tackle real-world challenges and meet the evolving needs of communities near and far. Applicants can choose from 20+ majors in community and rural development, environment and natural resources, food and nutrition, communication, applied economics, agriculture, international programs, and life sciences.

Applicants to Cornell CALS apply directly to one of our 20+ majors in the life, agricultural, environmental, and social sciences. If admitted, you will jump right into your major and gain access to a community of fellow students, faculty, and staff who share your interests. While your major will serve as your initial academic grounding point, your CALS experience will be flexible and exploratory as you take coursework across disciplines, participate in experiential learning, and develop new skills.

It is normal to have some trouble selecting one major. Our strongest CALS candidates have a multitude of interests, many of which are housed in more than one CALS major. As you approach the process of choosing a major, consider asking yourself the following questions:

Can you recall which classes, subject matters, assignments, and/or projects were most interesting to you? Can you identify common themes?

What extracurricular activities (clubs, hobbies, organizations, volunteering, work, etc.) bring you the most joy, fulfillment, and curiosity? Which ones do you find yourself looking forward to each year?

Is there a particular career, job, field, or educational goal you have in mind for yourself?

Students who thrive at CALS are eager to dive into subjects they love while staying open to discovering new interests. Start exploring CALS majors to answer these questions and more. The CALS Admissions team is here and eager to help. Please contact us if you need more information or have specific questions at cals_admissions@cornell.edu.

CALS seeks students who maintain a rigorous high school curriculum and demonstrate an outstanding record of academic achievement.

Biology and chemistry are required; physics is strongly recommended.**

A fourth unit of science is also strongly recommended.

*Calculus is required for Atmospheric Science, Biological Engineering, Biological Sciences, Earth & Climate Sciences, Environmental Engineering, Food Science, Global & Public Health Sciences, Information Science, Nutritional Sciences, and Statistics & Biometry majors.

**Physics is required for Atmospheric Science, Biological Engineering, Earth & Climate Sciences, and Environmental Engineering majors.

We recognize that high school curricula differ widely across schools, states, and countries.

The CALS Admissions team evaluates each applicant’s academic preparation within the context of their unique educational environment. This includes, but is not limited to, curriculum types such as AP, IB, Honors, dual enrollment, GCSE/A-Level and other international programs; academic calendar systems like quarter, semester, trimester, and block schedules; as well as the specific coursework available and any scheduling constraints communicated.

The goal of providing academic requirements by major is to ensure that students are academically prepared for success at Cornell CALS. However, given the variation in educational contexts, the Admissions team always seeks to understand how a student demonstrates readiness—even if their academic program doesn’t align exactly with our stated requirements.

By applying to Cornell’s College of Agriculture and Life Sciences (CALS), you are also applying directly to one of our majors. From here, you would be part of a community dedicated to purpose-driven science; working within your major and across disciplines to tackle complex, real-world challenges. Please respond to the following writing prompts when completing your Common Application Writing Supplement:

What interests you about the major you selected? Tell us about experiences (both in and outside of the classroom), questions, and goals that influenced your choice. (350-word limit)

What draws you to Cornell’s College of Agriculture and Life Sciences (CALS)? Reflect on the learning, community, or impact opportunities that matter most to you, and share how they align with Cornell CALS (as you understand it currently). (350-word limit)

What do we look for when reviewing your application? Here are a few important factors:

Academic Potential and Readiness. Have you challenged yourself in the curriculum and opportunities available to you? How have you demonstrated your passion for learning? Has your coursework prepared you for your academic interests at Cornell?

Character. Honesty. Open-mindedness. Initiative. Collaboration. Empathy. Curiosity. Your values are important to Cornell. Do your application essays and recommendations reflect your strongest personal attributes?

Involvement. Community engagement. Extracurricular activities. Work experience. Research. Leadership. Family responsibilities. What interests, special commitments, or talents have you developed? What have you spent time outside of the classroom doing?

Your Reasons for Choosing Cornell. How do you plan to take advantage of Cornell's exceptional learning environment? Why is it the right place for you?

Your reasons for choosing CALS. How is CALS a good fit for your academic interests? How are you a good fit for the college?

As you approach the application itself, we ask that you keep the following things in mind:

When selecting your teacher recommenders, please know that CALS does not have specific criteria or preferences. The teachers you choose do not necessarily need to have known you very long nor teach a subject matter that aligns with your desired CALS major. The letters of recommendation allow the admissions committee to learn more about you, your academic skill set, and the ways in which you collaborate and engage with others in a classroom setting. We encourage you to ask your recommenders early in the year to give them ample time to write a thoughtful letter.

Current CALS students, particularly the CALS Ambassadors, can help bring the Cornell experience to life and get you excited about the possibilities here on campus and beyond. Whether you engage with them through email, virtual events, in-person information sessions, blog posts, or social media, student voices can be a helpful resource as you contemplate your reasons for applying to CALS.

Seek out guidance from the CALS Admissions team, your high school counselors, and teachers as well as other adults in your life wherever it might benefit you in the application process.

Before you press submit, it is best to proofread each section of the application. Should you need to make a revision to your application or add new information, you will have the opportunity to do so in the Cornell Applicant Portal after submission.
